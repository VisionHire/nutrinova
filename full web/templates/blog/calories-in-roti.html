    If you eat roti every day — and most Indians do — you have probably wondered: How many calories are in roti? Whether you are trying to lose weight, track your meals, or simply eat healthier, knowing the calories in roti is a great first step.

    The good news? Roti is one of the most balanced foods in the Indian diet. It gives you energy, keeps you full, and is easy to fit into almost any health goal. In this guide, we will cover everything you need to know — from roti calories and nutrition to how it compares with rice and what it means for your weight loss journey.

    Let's dive in.

    How Many Calories Are in One Roti?

    A standard medium-sized roti made with whole wheat flour (atta) and no added oil or ghee contains approximately 70–120 calories. The exact number depends on the size, thickness, and ingredients used.

    Here is a quick overview of calories in one roti based on common types:

    Type of Roti Size Calories (approx.)
    Plain wheat roti (no ghee) Medium (30g) 70–80 kcal
    Plain wheat roti (with ghee) Medium (30g) 100–120 kcal
    Multigrain roti Medium (30g) 75–90 kcal
    Bajra (pearl millet) roti Medium (35g) 90–100 kcal
    Maida (all-purpose flour) roti Medium (30g) 80–90 kcal
    Jowar (sorghum) roti Medium (35g) 85–95 kcal
    Practical example: If you eat 2 medium wheat rotis with a teaspoon of ghee each, you are consuming roughly 220–260 calories from the rotis alone. This is a helpful number to keep in mind when planning your daily Indian diet calories.

    Nutritional Value of Roti

    Beyond just calories in roti, it is important to understand what those calories are made of. One medium whole wheat roti (approx. 30g, no added fat) offers the following:

    Nutrient Amount per Roti
    Calories 70–80 kcal
    Carbohydrates 14–16g
    Protein 2–3g
    Dietary Fibre 1–2g
    Fat 0.4–1g
    Iron ~0.5mg
    Calcium ~10mg
    Phosphorus ~40mg

    Calories

    Most of the calories in roti come from carbohydrates. These are complex carbs, which means they digest slowly and give you steady energy throughout the day — unlike sugary snacks that cause a quick spike and crash.

    Protein

    Whole wheat roti provides a moderate amount of plant-based protein. When paired with dal, paneer, or a legume-based sabzi, your meal becomes a complete protein source. This is the foundation of a balanced Indian vegetarian diet.

    Carbohydrates

    Roti is primarily a carbohydrate food. The carbs in whole wheat roti are complex in nature, meaning they have a lower glycaemic impact compared to refined grains. This helps in managing energy levels and hunger.

    Fiber

    Whole wheat roti is a decent source of dietary fibre, especially if the bran is retained in the flour. Fibre supports digestion, keeps you feeling full for longer, and supports healthy blood sugar management.

    Fat

    A plain roti without ghee or oil is very low in fat. The fat content increases significantly when ghee or butter is added — something to keep in mind if you are monitoring your calorie intake.

    Factors That Affect Calories in Roti

    The nutrition in roti is not fixed. Several factors can raise or lower the calorie count considerably.

    Size

    This is the biggest variable. A small roti (25g) might have 55–65 calories, while a large one (50g) can cross 150 calories. Restaurant rotis or tawa rotis made at dhabas are often much larger than homemade ones.

    Thickness

    A thicker roti uses more dough and naturally has more calories. Thin, phulka-style rotis are lighter and lower in calories than thick, stuffed parathas.

    Flour Type

    • Whole wheat (atta): Best for nutrition, higher fibre, ~70–80 kcal
    • Maida (refined flour): Lower fibre, slightly higher calories, less filling
    • Bajra, jowar, ragi: Higher in minerals and fibre; calorie counts vary but are comparable to wheat
    • Multigrain: Often similar to whole wheat, with added micronutrients

    Added Oil or Ghee

    This is where roti calories can jump significantly. One teaspoon of ghee adds about 45 calories. If you apply ghee on both sides, you can easily add 90 extra calories per roti. Many people underestimate this.

    Tip: If you love ghee on your roti, use just half a teaspoon and savour it — you will still get the flavour while keeping roti calories in check.

    Is Roti Good for Weight Loss?

    Yes — roti can absolutely be part of a weight loss diet, provided you are mindful of portions and preparation.

    Here is why roti works well for weight management:

    • It keeps you full. The complex carbs and fibre in whole wheat roti slow digestion and reduce hunger, helping you avoid overeating at the next meal.
    • It is naturally low in fat. A plain roti has less than 1g of fat. Compared to fried snacks or processed foods, it is a much better choice for Indian diet calories management.
    • It is versatile. Roti pairs beautifully with vegetables, lentils, and lean proteins — making it easy to build a balanced, calorie-appropriate meal.
    • It is portion-friendly. Unlike rice, where it is harder to judge how much you are eating, rotis are easy to count. Two or three rotis per meal is a practical, trackable serving.

    What to watch out for: The challenge with roti and weight loss comes from generous amounts of ghee, pairing with high-calorie gravies, or eating very large rotis. Keep an eye on add-ons, not just the roti itself.

    Roti vs Rice Calories

    One of the most common questions in Indian nutrition circles is: which is better for weight loss — roti or rice?

    Food Serving Calories Fibre Protein
    Wheat roti (plain) 1 medium (30g) 70–80 kcal 1–2g 2–3g
    White rice (cooked) 1 small katori (100g) 130–140 kcal 0.3g 2.7g
    Brown rice (cooked) 1 small katori (100g) 110–120 kcal 1.6g 2.6g

    At first glance, roti has fewer calories per serving than a katori of rice. Roti also has more fibre, which supports satiety and digestion. However, this comparison is not entirely fair because people typically eat more rotis or a larger rice serving.

    The honest answer: neither is bad. Both roti and rice are staples of the Indian diet and can be part of a healthy eating plan. What matters more is your total daily calorie intake, your physical activity, and how you build your whole meal.

    If you are closely tracking Indian diet calories, roti may be slightly easier to portion-control since you count individual pieces.

    Benefits of Eating Roti

    Roti is not just a calorie source — it comes with several genuine health benefits when made with quality ingredients:

    • Supports digestion. The fibre in whole wheat roti feeds healthy gut bacteria and promotes regular bowel movements.
    • Provides sustained energy. Complex carbohydrates in roti release energy slowly, making it a practical choice for an active lifestyle.
    • Rich in B vitamins. Whole wheat flour contains thiamine, niacin, and folate — vitamins that support metabolism and overall health.
    • Contains important minerals. Roti provides iron, phosphorus, and magnesium, which are essential for everyday body functions.
    • Versatile and accessible. Roti is easy to make, affordable, and pairs with almost every Indian dish — making it one of the most practical healthy foods available.
    • Gluten-free alternatives available. For those who cannot eat wheat, ragi, jowar, and bajra rotis offer excellent nutrition without gluten.

    Who Should Eat Roti?

    Roti is suitable for most people and can be adapted for different health goals:

    • For weight loss: Choose whole wheat or multigrain roti, limit ghee, and pair with vegetables and dal for a filling, balanced meal.
    • For fitness and muscle building: Combine roti with a high-protein sabzi like paneer, chana, or eggs to support muscle repair and growth.
    • For diabetes management: Opt for roti made from ragi, bajra, or jowar, as these have a lower glycaemic index than refined wheat flour. Always consult a dietitian for personalised advice.
    • For children: Roti provides energy and nutrients that growing children need. Small, soft rotis with ghee and dal make an excellent meal.
    • For elderly individuals: Soft, thin rotis are easy to chew and digest. Whole grain options support gut health and mineral intake.
    • For vegetarians and vegans: Roti is naturally plant-based and an excellent everyday staple.

    Conclusion

    Understanding the calories in roti is a simple but powerful step towards better eating. A plain medium wheat roti contains around 70–80 calories, making it one of the most practical and nutritious staple foods in the Indian diet. When made with whole grains and eaten with mindful portions, roti supports digestion, provides steady energy, and fits comfortably into a weight loss or fitness plan.

    The key is not to fear roti — but to be aware of what you add to it and how much you eat. Track your portions, watch the ghee, choose whole grain flours, and pair it with nutritious accompaniments.

    Every healthy journey begins with small, consistent choices. Knowing your roti calories is one of them.
